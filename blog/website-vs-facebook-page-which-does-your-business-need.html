Website vs Facebook Page: Which Does Your Kenyan Business Need?

"I already have a Facebook page, do I really need a website?" is one of the most common questions we get. Here's the honest answer, and why it usually isn't either/or.

A Facebook page is a good start. It was never meant to be the whole plan

Setting up a Facebook or Instagram page is free, fast, and genuinely useful for reaching people who already follow you. That's exactly why so many businesses in Kenya start there and stop. But a page comes with limits that only become obvious once a business starts trying to grow: you don't own it, its reach is controlled by an algorithm that changes without notice, and it's not built to be found by someone who has never heard of your business before.

What a Facebook page genuinely can't do

  • Rank on Google. Someone searching "web design Nairobi" or "best caterer in Kiambu" is very unlikely to land on your Facebook page — that's a website's job.
  • Survive an account issue. Pages get restricted, hacked, or disabled with little warning, and support is slow to nonexistent. A website is yours; nobody can take it down over a policy dispute.
  • Look credible to a first-time visitor. A page with three-year-old cover photos and a scattering of posts quietly signals a business that might not be very active — even if it is.
  • Be cited by AI assistants confidently. When someone asks ChatGPT or Gemini to recommend a business, those tools lean on structured, consistent information — the kind a real website provides, not a social feed.
  • Do more than post updates. An online store, a booking form, service pages that actually explain what you do — none of that fits inside a Facebook page.

Where a Facebook or Instagram page still earns its place

Social media is genuinely good at what it does: staying visible to people who already know you, sharing quick updates, and building familiarity through consistent posting. The two aren't competitors — a Facebook or Instagram page brings people in, and a website is where they actually make a decision. That's exactly the reasoning behind pairing our web design work with social media management: one drives awareness, the other converts it.

The honest answer

If you're testing an idea with zero budget, a Facebook page is a reasonable place to start. But the moment you're serious about being found by people who don't already know you, relying on a page alone is a ceiling on how much the business can grow — and it's a ceiling that costs surprisingly little to remove.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Facebook page enough for a small business in Kenya?

A Facebook page can work as a starting point, but it has real limits: you don't own it, its reach depends on an algorithm you don't control, and it can't be found by anyone searching on Google. A website is what you own, what ranks in search, and what an AI assistant can actually cite when recommending a business.

Can Google find my Facebook page instead of a website?

Rarely for anything beyond your business name. Facebook pages are not built to rank for the searches that actually bring in new customers, like "web design Nairobi" or "best caterer in Kiambu" — a proper website with real content is what ranks for those.

Do I need a website if my customers mostly find me through WhatsApp and Instagram?

Social media is great for reaching people who already know you exist. A website is what convinces someone who's never heard of you before, and what search engines and AI assistants point to when someone asks for a recommendation without knowing your name at all.

What can a website do that a Facebook page can't?

A website ranks on Google, works even if your social account is restricted or disabled, gives you full control over design and content, and can include features social media can't, like an online store, service pages, or a booking form.
