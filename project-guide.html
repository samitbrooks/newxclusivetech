From brief to handover

  1. Scope and written quote. Agree pages, integrations, content, timetable, revision rounds and payment milestones.
  2. Deposit and project inputs. The existing terms require a 50% deposit before work begins. Supply approved content, brand assets and any required account access.
  3. Design review. Review the proposed layout and consolidate feedback within the agreed revision rounds.
  4. Development and testing. Check mobile layouts, forms, content and any payment integrations against the agreed acceptance criteria.
  5. Approval and handover. Review the completed work, settle the remaining balance under the agreed terms, then launch or receive the files and access details.

Typical delivery is 3–5 business days for a landing page, 2–3 weeks for Starter, 3–5 weeks for Growth and 5–7 weeks for e-commerce. The confirmed schedule depends on scope, content readiness and approval turnaround.

What happens after launch?

Included launch support

Starter includes one month, Growth three months and e-commerce six months of support. Your quote sets the scope of fixes and assistance included.

Care after launch

Choose an optional care plan from KES 2,000/month for four hours of updates and support. Request a separate quote for larger changes or new functionality.

Ownership and access

On full payment, receive the agreed project files and access details. Third-party software, fonts, stock assets and reusable tools remain subject to their licences and the agreed terms.

Agree the terms before work starts

  • Payment: 50% deposit before work; the remaining balance is due on delivery, before publication or file transfer, under the current terms.
  • Revisions: Included rounds are specified in the quote. Changes outside the agreed scope are quoted separately.
  • Cancellation: Work completed is invoiced; the deposit is non-refundable once design work begins, as set out in the existing terms.
  • Ownership: The project agreement distinguishes your delivered work from third-party licences and reusable tools.

Read the full terms before accepting a quote. Ask us to clarify anything your project agreement needs to cover.

Questions to ask before choosing a developer

Can you redesign my existing website?

Yes. Start with a review of your current pages, domain, forms and integrations. We agree what will be retained, replaced and redirected before migration.

Can I keep my domain and hosting?

Bring your existing details to the project review. We confirm access, compatibility and any migration work before recommending changes; you do not have to buy a new domain just to discuss a redesign.

What is needed for M-Pesa integration?

We review your merchant account, the payment flow and the appropriate API access with you. Merchant approval and payment-provider fees are separate from the website build.

Who supplies the words and photographs?

Your quote states whether you provide approved content and licensed images or whether content production is included as a separate service. Content readiness affects the launch schedule.

Can I update the website after launch?

For projects that include a CMS, handover covers the agreed editing workflow. Confirm any training, maintenance or ongoing editing requirement in the scope.

Do you guarantee first-page rankings or sales?

No. On-page SEO helps search engines access and understand a site, but indexing, rankings, enquiries and sales depend on factors beyond a website build. We agree measurable work and review actual performance.

How do I compare two website quotes?

Compare page count, integrations, revision rounds, content responsibilities, domain and hosting renewals, support, ownership and acceptance criteria—not only the headline price.

What happens if I want more features later?

Agree a separate scope and quote before extra work begins. Ask whether an existing upgrade offer applies to your project; do not assume that every new feature is included in the original fee.
