The unusual path to the web

How a science degree turned into a career in web design

Between 2018 and 2024, I studied for a Bachelor of Pharmacy (B.Pharm) at Kenyatta University — a rigorous, science-heavy program that had me elbow-deep in dispensing, patient counselling, clinical rotations and inventory management across hospital and community pharmacy settings. It's precise, high-stakes work: get a dosage wrong and it isn't a bug you can patch later.

What most people didn't know is that throughout every semester of that degree, I was quietly teaching myself to design and build websites. It started as curiosity — HTML and CSS on the side — and turned into a genuine passion for UI/UX design, front-end development and graphic design that I never grew out of. By the time I graduated, I wasn't choosing between two paths. I was already walking both.

Today I practice as a registered Pharmacist while also running Xclusive Tech, designing and developing websites for businesses across Kenya. I'm not treating one as a fallback for the other — I actively do both, and each one makes me better at the other.

"Pharmacy taught me precision. Tech gave me a canvas for it."

How I work

Habits from the pharmacy bench that carry over into every project

Precision by default

Dispensing medication leaves no room for "close enough." I bring that same zero-tolerance-for-error mindset to code reviews, testing and pixel-perfect layouts.

Useful over flashy

A site that looks impressive but confuses visitors isn't doing its job. I design for clarity and conversion first, decoration second.

Clear communication

Years of patient counselling trained me to explain complicated things simply. I bring that same clarity to client conversations — no unnecessary jargon.

Always learning

I taught myself this entire craft alongside a demanding science degree. That habit hasn't stopped — from AI-assisted development to new frameworks, I keep adding to the toolkit.
